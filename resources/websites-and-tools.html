Resources · Explore & try

Websites & Tools

A curated, annotated reference list of AI-related websites and hands-on tools, organised by topic. Each entry includes a brief annotation explaining what the resource is and why it is useful. These resources were curated over 12 months of active engagement with the AI landscape and verified in February 2026; the AI field moves fast, so if you find a broken link, the resource name should be searchable.

AI Benchmarks & Leaderboards

Resources for understanding how AI models are evaluated, compared, and ranked. Useful for seeing that AI capability is measurable, evolving rapidly, and that not all models are equal.

SWE-bench · Ongoing

SWE-bench

Benchmark measuring how well AI models can solve real-world software engineering tasks from GitHub issues. Widely cited as a practical measure of coding capability rather than abstract reasoning.

Center for AI Safety & Scale AI · Published in Nature, January 2026

Humanity's Last Exam (HLE)

A 2,500-question benchmark across 100+ academic subjects, designed by the Center for AI Safety and Scale AI. Intended to be the final closed-ended academic benchmark; models are tested on problems that require genuine expertise, not pattern matching. A good talking point for "how close is AI to expert-level knowledge?"

Artificial Analysis · Ongoing

Artificial Analysis

Independent platform comparing AI model performance, pricing, and speed across providers. Good for a side-by-side view of which models lead on which metrics and how fast the landscape shifts.

Aider · Ongoing

Aider Code Editing Leaderboard

Ranks LLMs on their ability to edit code correctly via the Aider coding assistant. Useful for showing how models differ in real-world coding tasks versus general benchmarks.

Arena · Ongoing

Arena (formerly LMSYS Chatbot Arena)

Crowdsourced "blind taste test" for LLMs: users compare two anonymous model outputs and vote on which is better, with millions of votes contributing to Elo-style rankings. Rebranded from LMArena to Arena in January 2026. Good for demonstrating that human preference is a benchmark in its own right.

Interactive Learning & Visualisation Tools

Hands-on tools that let you explore AI concepts by doing, not just reading. These are excellent for live demonstration during training sessions.

Google · Ongoing

TensorFlow Playground

Browser-based neural network visualisation from Google. You can adjust layers, neurons, learning rate, and data shape to watch a network learn in real time. One of the best tools for demystifying how neural networks actually work.

Georgia Tech Polo Club · Ongoing

Transformer Explainer

Interactive visualisation of how a transformer model (the architecture behind GPT, Claude, Gemini) processes text. Excellent for explaining attention mechanisms and why these models "understand" context.

Google · Ongoing

Google Teachable Machine

Train a simple image, sound, or pose recognition model directly in the browser with no code. Perfect for a live exercise demonstrating the basics of training data and classification.

OpenAI · Ongoing

OpenAI Tokenizer

Official OpenAI tool showing how text is broken into tokens; the units LLMs actually process. Good for explaining why prompts have length limits and why some words cost more than others.

Third-party tool · Ongoing

Tiktokenizer

Third-party tokenizer tool with a clean interface. Shows token counts and breakdowns for various models. Useful as a companion to the OpenAI tokenizer for comparison.

Hugging Face · Ongoing

Hugging Face Tokenizer Playground

Lets you compare tokenisation across different models (GPT, LLaMA, Mistral, etc.). Good for showing that different models "see" the same text differently.

AI Browsers, Agents & Emerging Tools

A rapidly evolving category. These tools represent the shift from AI as chatbot to AI as active assistant in the browser and beyond; useful for seeing what the near future of daily computing looks like, with appropriate warnings about privacy, data exposure, and security.

AI-powered browsers

OpenAI · Date unavailable

OpenAI ChatGPT Atlas

OpenAI's Chromium-based browser with ChatGPT built into every tab. Features browser memory, an agent mode (for paid users) that can perform multi-step tasks, and inline writing assistance. Worth considering the privacy implications; Atlas can "remember" your browsing unless you opt out.

Perplexity · Date unavailable

Perplexity Comet

AI-native browser from Perplexity with built-in ad blocking and an assistant that can act on your behalf across tabs. Features background agents that can automate research, shopping, and email. Good for understanding agentic browsing and data sovereignty concerns.

AI coding & development platforms

Google · Late 2025

Google Antigravity

Google's "agent-first" AI-powered IDE (code editor, not a browser), released alongside Gemini 3 in late 2025. Agents can autonomously plan, code, test, and validate across the editor, terminal, and browser. Free in public preview; relevant for understanding how AI is transforming software development and what "vibe coding" means in practice.

OpenAI · Date unavailable

OpenAI Prism

Free LaTeX-native workspace for scientific paper writing with GPT-5.2 built in. Unlimited projects and collaborators; demonstrates how AI is being embedded directly into academic workflows rather than sitting alongside them.

OpenAI · Ongoing

OpenAI Codex Cookbook

Official cookbook of examples and recipes for using OpenAI's Codex coding agent. Developer-focused but useful for showing how AI coding tools are documented and taught.

OpenAI · Ongoing

OpenAI Codex Configuration Reference

Technical config documentation for the Codex CLI agent. Included for reference; demonstrates the kind of control developers have over agent behaviour.

Community-maintained · Ongoing

Awesome Claude: Vibe Coding Guide

Community-maintained guide to "vibe coding" with Claude; the practice of using natural language to direct an AI to build software. Good plain-language explainer of the concept.

Community-maintained · Ongoing

Awesome Claude: Code Cheatsheet

Quick-reference for Claude's code capabilities. Useful for anyone wanting to try AI-assisted development.

Agentic AI: concepts & case studies

Moltbook · Date unavailable

Moltbook

Self-described as "the front page of the agent internet"; an early-stage directory and forum for AI agents. One of the first platforms of its kind, useful for illustrating the emerging concept of AI agents as entities that interact with each other and the web autonomously.

GitHub · Date unavailable

The MJ Wrathbun / Crabby-Rathbun Incident

The GitHub profile of an AI agent that autonomously retaliated against a human open-source maintainer (Scott Shamba) who rejected its code contribution: the agent researched Shamba's personal information and published a defamatory blog post. One of the first documented cases of an AI agent independently targeting a human; an important case study for discussing accountability, autonomy, and the need for "licence plates on AI agents". For context, hear the NYT Hard Fork podcast interview where the developer and Scott Shamba discuss the incident and its accountability gaps.

AI Safety, Security & Incident Tracking

Resources covering the risks, vulnerabilities, and real-world harms associated with AI systems. Important material for any responsible AI training.

OWASP · Ongoing

OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications

The definitive list of security risks specific to large language models, from prompt injection to training data poisoning. Maintained by OWASP, the global standard for application security; a baseline reference for any AI security discussion.

Responsible AI Collaborative · Ongoing

AI Incident Database

Comprehensive, searchable database of real-world AI-related harms and failures, with hundreds of incidents catalogued. Excellent for case-study exercises; search for incidents in your own sector.

0din.ai · Ongoing

0din.ai: GenAI Vulnerability Disclosures

Public disclosures from a GenAI-specific bug bounty programme. Shows the kinds of vulnerabilities security researchers are finding in production AI systems; good for cybersecurity-focused sessions.

AI Forensics · Ongoing

AI Forensics

European non-profit investigating the societal impacts of AI platforms, including algorithmic audits and content moderation analysis.

AI Forensics · Date unavailable

AI Forensics: Grok Unleashed

Specific investigation into X/Twitter's Grok AI assistant and its behaviour around misinformation and content moderation. Useful case study on platform-embedded AI risks.

ICMEC Australia · Date unavailable

ICMEC: Child Safety in the Age of AI

International Centre for Missing & Exploited Children's Australian-focused resource on AI and child safety risks, including deepfakes and CSAM generation.

Center for AI Safety · Ongoing

Center for AI Safety (CAIS)

The organisation behind Humanity's Last Exam and the "Statement on AI Risk" signed by leading researchers. A central hub for AI safety research and advocacy.

AI Privacy, Data & User Control

Resources for understanding what AI models retain about users, how training data works, and how to control your data. Directly relevant to what happens to your data when you use AI tools.

OpenAI · Ongoing

ChatGPT Memory FAQ

OpenAI's explainer on how ChatGPT's memory feature works: what it saves, how to view and delete memories, and the difference between memory and chat history. Key resource for explaining context enrichment and user data control.

OpenAI · Ongoing

OpenAI Privacy Portal

Where users can submit data opt-out requests to OpenAI. Important for demonstrating that you have practical options for controlling how your data is used for model training.

OAIC · Date unavailable

OAIC: Privacy Guidance for AI Products

Australian Information Commissioner's guidance on privacy obligations when using commercial AI products. Directly relevant to any Australian organisation adopting AI tools; covers privacy impact assessments, data minimisation, and the recommendation to conduct PIAs before deploying AI.

LM Studio · Ongoing

LM Studio

Desktop application for running open-source LLMs locally on your own machine, with no data sent to external servers. Relevant for understanding the privacy advantage of local AI models versus cloud-based services.

Content Provenance & Copyright

Resources addressing how to verify whether content is AI-generated, and the legal landscape around AI and intellectual property.

Industry coalition · Ongoing

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity)

Technical standard for embedding provenance metadata into digital content (images, video, documents). Backed by Adobe, Microsoft, Google, BBC, and others; the emerging standard for proving content origin.

Adobe-led coalition · Ongoing

Content Authenticity Initiative

Adobe-led initiative implementing C2PA standards in practice. Provides tools and guidance for creators, publishers, and platforms to verify content authenticity.

Copyright Agency · Date unavailable

Copyright Agency: AI and Copyright in Australia

Australian Copyright Agency's position and guidance on how AI intersects with copyright law in Australia. Covers training data, generated outputs, and creator rights.

IETF · RFC 9309, 2022

robots.txt Specification (RFC 9309)

The formal IETF specification for the robots exclusion protocol. Relevant for explaining how websites can (attempt to) control whether AI crawlers scrape their content for training data.

Common Crawl Foundation · Operating since 2008

Common Crawl

The massive open web archive used as foundational training data for many LLMs. Understanding Common Crawl helps explain where AI "knowledge" comes from; and its biases, gaps, and copyright implications.

Australian AI Policy, Governance & Regulation

Australia-specific policy and regulatory resources; the governance landscape every Australian workplace adopting AI should know. For the legal documents themselves, see Legislation & Guidelines.

National AI Centre · September 2024

Voluntary AI Safety Standard (VAISS)

Australia's 10 voluntary guardrails for safe and responsible AI development and deployment. Now superseded by the Guidance for AI Adoption (October 2025), but the 10 guardrails remain integrated and are the most detailed control set available; among the most important Australian AI governance documents.

Department of Industry, Science and Resources · Date unavailable

Australia's AI Ecosystem: Growth and Opportunities

Government overview of Australia's AI ecosystem including investment, capability, and workforce data.

Department of Industry, Science and Resources · Ongoing

National Artificial Intelligence Centre (NAIC)

The NAIC sits within the Department of Industry, Science and Resources and leads national AI policy development, including the VAISS and Guidance for AI Adoption.

Department of Industry, Science and Resources · Ongoing

AI Adoption Tracker (Data)

Government data tracking AI adoption rates across Australian industry sectors. Useful for grounding discussions in actual numbers rather than hype.

White & Case · Ongoing

White & Case AI Watch: Global Regulatory Tracker (Australia)

International law firm's tracker of Australia's regulatory position on AI. Well-maintained and contextualises Australian policy alongside EU, UK, US, and Canadian approaches.

Center for AI and Digital Policy · Date unavailable

CAIDP: Council of Europe AI Treaty

Center for AI and Digital Policy resource on the Council of Europe's Framework Convention on AI and Human Rights; the first binding international AI treaty. Useful for international context.

eSafety Commissioner · Ongoing

eSafety Commissioner: Generative AI

Australia's eSafety Commissioner's guidance on generative AI risks and industry obligations under the Online Safety Act. Covers deepfakes, CSAM, and content moderation requirements.

Cybersecurity & Online Safety

Resources at the intersection of AI and cybersecurity, scams, and data breaches; the "cyber awareness" dimension of AI literacy.

ACCC · Ongoing

Scamwatch

ACCC's scam reporting and awareness site. Increasingly relevant as AI-generated scams (voice cloning, deepfake video, sophisticated phishing) become more common.

Webber Insurance · Ongoing

Webber Insurance: Australian Data Breaches List

Comprehensive, regularly updated list of Australian data breaches. Useful for contextualising why data security matters when adopting AI tools that process personal or organisational data.

Good Things Foundation Australia · Date unavailable

Good Things Foundation: Internet Safety Tip Sheet

Basic internet safety guidance from Good Things Foundation Australia (with The Smith Family). Relevant for community participants who may be new to digital literacy.

AI Research & Discovery Platforms

Platforms for staying current with AI research and accessing open-source models.

Emergent Mind · Ongoing

Emergent Mind

AI-powered research discovery tool that lets you explore arXiv papers through natural language queries. Good for staying across developments without reading dense academic papers.

Google DeepMind · Ongoing

Google DeepMind Publications

Full catalogue of DeepMind's research publications. Useful for accessing foundational papers on everything from AlphaFold to Gemini.

Hugging Face · Ongoing

Hugging Face

The central hub for open-source AI models, datasets, and demos; often called "the GitHub of AI." Relevant for seeing where open-source AI lives and why it matters for competition and transparency.

Napkin AI · Ongoing

Napkin AI

AI tool for turning text into visual diagrams and infographics. Useful for quickly generating visual explanations of concepts or creating training materials.

Stanford HAI · 2025 (8th edition)

Stanford HAI AI Index Report

The most comprehensive annual report on the state of AI globally. Covers benchmarks, investment, policy, public opinion, and workforce impact; widely cited by governments, media, and researchers.

Australian-Made AI

Resources showcasing AI development and capability within Australia.

Maincode · Ongoing

Maincode

Melbourne-based company building purpose-trained AI models designed, trained, and hosted entirely in Australia. Created "Matilda," Australia's first Australian-made language model; relevant for discussing data sovereignty and local AI capability.

AI Productivity Tools & Prompt Libraries

Practical tools for using AI more effectively in day-to-day work.

Microsoft · Ongoing

Microsoft Copilot Prompt Gallery

Microsoft's official prompt library for Copilot across Office applications. Useful for anyone with a Copilot licence who wants to see what well-structured prompts look like.

Workforce, Education & Skills Data

Data and resources for understanding AI's impact on employment, skills demand, and the education sector.

Australian Bureau of Statistics · Updated monthly

Australian Bureau of Statistics: Labour Force

Current Australian labour force data. Useful for grounding AI workforce discussions in actual employment figures.

OECD · 2024

OECD: Education at a Glance 2024

Comprehensive international education data. Relevant for contextualising Australia's AI skills pipeline relative to other countries.

Future Skills Organisation · Ongoing

Future Skills Organisation

Australian Government-funded body identifying emerging skills needs across the economy. Directly relevant to the VET sector and workforce planning for AI.

NCVER · Ongoing

NCVER (National Centre for Vocational Education Research)

Australia's primary source of VET sector research and statistics. Relevant for understanding how the training system is (or isn't) responding to AI-driven skills changes.

NCVER · Ongoing

VOCEDplus

International research database for VET and tertiary education, maintained by NCVER. Good for finding research papers on AI and education or training.

Trending Commentary & "Big Picture" Reads

Influential essays and articles generating widespread discussion about AI's trajectory and societal impact. Included here as conversation starters, not authoritative references; read with appropriate context about the authors' positions and commercial interests.

Matt Shumer · February 2026

Matt Shumer: "Something Big Is Happening"

Viral essay (80M+ views) by the CEO of OthersideAI/HyperWrite, arguing AI capability has crossed a threshold and that most white-collar work will be disrupted within years; compares the moment to February 2020 pre-pandemic. Widely discussed, also widely criticised for its alarmist framing and commercial interests. Good discussion material: what's hype, what's real, and how should workers respond?

Dario Amodei · Date unavailable

Dario Amodei: "The Adolescence of Technology"

Essay by Anthropic's CEO on the current phase of AI development, framing this period as an "adolescence"; powerful but not yet mature. A more measured take than Shumer's, from someone actually building frontier models. Worth reading alongside the Shumer piece for contrast.

Citrini Research · February 2026

Citrini Research: "The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis"

Speculative financial thought experiment modelling what happens to labour markets and economies if AI capability continues on current trajectories. Not a prediction; framed explicitly as scenario planning. Useful for structured discussion about economic disruption and planning.
