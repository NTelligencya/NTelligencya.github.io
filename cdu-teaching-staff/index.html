AI Workshop for Teaching and Student-facing Support Staff

Students are already using generative AI, and the question is no longer whether to permit it but how to design assessment that remains defensible when they do. This workshop is for higher education and TAFE lecturers, and for professional staff who work directly with CDU students; anyone who wants to lift their understanding of how students are using AI.

About this workshop

We cover the most recent data on what students are actually doing with these tools; how software claiming to detect AI writing works, and where it doesn't; where detection sits as evidence; and workable AI use declarations to accompany assessments. We look at what the pedagogy literature says about the cognitive outsourcing that comes with AI tool use, and experiment with tools marketed as learning instruments, including Copilot's Study and Learn agent. We then turn to redesigning assessment tasks so the evidence of learning holds up, anchored by TEQSA's guidance on assessment reform and ASQA's draft principles for the responsible use of AI.

No technical background assumed. Bring your ideas and insights to share with colleagues, or an assessment task you are uneasy about. A laptop is essential.
