§ Heading styles

Styles: the fix for that

On the Home tab, the Styles gallery holds named looks: Normal, Heading 1, Heading 2. A style changes how a line looks and tells Word what the line is.

these two are the only styles this lesson needs Heading 1 marks the big sections; Heading 2 marks the parts inside them

Bold text says nothing

Making a line big and bold changes pixels, not meaning. Word still files it under "just text".

A heading style says "I am a heading"

Once a line is styled Heading 1 or Heading 2, Word can list it, link to it, and number its page; that is exactly what a Table of Contents needs.

§ Make it stick

The whole skill, in three moves

  • Styles first. Give every heading a heading style: Heading 1 for the big sections, Heading 2 for the parts inside them. The Navigation pane is your preview of what Word can see.
  • Let Word type it. Put the cursor where the contents page belongs, then References ▸ Table of Contents ▸ an automatic table. Never the manual one.
  • Update before it leaves you. Before you print, send or save as PDF: click in the table, Update Table, Update entire table. Two clicks, and the snapshot is current.
Styles first. Let Word type it. Update before it leaves you.
§ Make it stick

The three usual trip-ups

"I bolded it, why isn't it listed?"

Bold text is decoration; Word cannot see it as a heading. Only lines styled Heading 1, 2 or 3 make it into the table.

Fix: click the line, apply a heading style, update the table.

"I updated, but it's still wrong"

"Update page numbers only" refreshes the numbers and nothing else; new and renamed headings stay missing.

Fix: when in doubt, always pick Update entire table.

"The PDF I sent shows the old contents"

Saving as PDF freezes whatever the table said at that moment. A stale snapshot travels with the file.

Fix: update the table, then export. In that order, every time.

All three have the same shape: the table only knows what you told it, when you last told it.
