News to keep an eye on: AI law in Australia

Where the AI rules are heading, and what it means for you right now.

  • In September 2024 the federal government proposed 10 mandatory guardrails for AI in high-risk settings.
  • On 2 December 2025 the National AI Plan (Minister Tim Ayres) decided not to proceed, for now, with those guardrails or a standalone AI Act. The approach is technology-neutral: build on the laws we already have, supported by voluntary guidance. (summary, minister's statement)
  • A new Australian AI Safety Institute launched in early 2026. It advises on AI risks; it does not enforce.
  • High-risk guardrails are accepted in principle for later, with more consultation, and a standards-led direction is emerging. This story is still moving, so check the latest before delivery.

What it means for you now: there is no AI-specific law yet, so your obligations come from laws that already exist, such as privacy under the Information Act 2002 (NT), record-keeping and work health and safety, and from the council's own AI policy. Follow policy and existing law today, and watch this space.
