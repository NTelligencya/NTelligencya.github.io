Excel: The Basics

No lookups, no fear. We start where a spreadsheet earns its keep: reading a big sheet with sort and filter, writing your first formulas, and understanding the one thing about dates that trips everyone up.

Session 4 running order

0:00

Recap, and what a spreadsheet is for

A spreadsheet is a tidy grid of rows and columns, and the one habit that saves the most grief: one thing per column, one record per row, and no blank rows in the middle of your data.

0:10

Reading a wall of rows: sort and filter

Two questions, two tools. Sort to put rows in order, filter to hide the rows you do not need right now, then answer real questions from the data.

0:35

Your first formulas

The everyday four: SUM to add up, AVERAGE to find the middle, the fill handle to copy a formula down a column, and what a cell reference means. Small wins that make a spreadsheet start working for you.

Break · 5 minutes

How Excel really sees dates

The fun one. A date in Excel is really a number: the count of days since 1 January 1900. That is why dates can be added and subtracted, and why a date typed as text sorts in the wrong order. Covers the serial number, and the format that sorts correctly every time.
