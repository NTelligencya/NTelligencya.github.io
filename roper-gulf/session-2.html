Word with Copilot, then Your Orientation Deck

Word is the calmest place to learn to work with AI. We get Copilot generating text, practise turning a rough ask into a good prompt, tidy the document with heading styles, and finish with the brief for your orientation-deck homework.

Recap, and why Copilot writes the way it does

A quick callback to last week: Copilot is predicting text from patterns, which is why it is a brilliant first-drafter, and why you always read what it gives you.

How AI Predicts the Next WordQuick revisit · new window
0:12

Copilot in Word: writing from a plain request

Using Copilot in a blank Word document to draft something real and useful: a short notice, a memo, a paragraph for a report. It produces a first draft in seconds, and that draft is a starting point, not the finished job.

0:35

Beginner prompting: the same task, a better prompt

Improving the ask. A good prompt says who you are, what you want, a little context, and the format. The weak prompt and the strong prompt side by side make the difference obvious.

Weak: write a notice about the depot closing early

Better: You are writing for Roper Gulf Regional Council staff. Draft a short, friendly notice that the Works Depot will close at 1pm this Friday for maintenance, who to contact for urgent jobs, and when it reopens. Keep it to four sentences in plain language.

Making it look right: headings and a table of contents

A little formatting goes a long way. Heading styles let Word see the structure of a document, and then a table of contents that builds and updates itself. The click-to-build tutorial walks through it.

1:25

Your homework: gather it, then build your orientation deck

The homework for the no-session week: build a short slide deck that introduces your workplace, the kind of thing you could show a new starter. The flyer below has the brief, and Copilot can draft the outline so nobody faces a blank screen.

You are helping me plan a short orientation presentation for a new starter in my team at Roper Gulf Regional Council. Draft an outline of about eight slides that introduces our workplace: the building and getting around, safety and who to call, who is who in the team, where we fit in the council, and my role. Give each slide a title and three short bullet points, in plain language.

Keep it safe: no personal details or community information goes into Copilot; first names or job titles only.
